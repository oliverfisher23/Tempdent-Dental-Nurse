### **1\. Briefing (about 50 seconds)**

Hello, my name’s Terence, and I'm the executive sous chef here at art'otel Hoxton, so I run the kitchen day to day. I’m really glad you've joined us.

*Big day*\! We've got a product launch event tonight. There are one hundred guests, and our client's been planning it for months. This means that our main priority is running this event properly, while still maintaining a proper service at the hotel. 

Normally as a new starter, you would work with one of our more junior chefs, known as the chefs de partie. Today, you're with me. I want to see how you think, not just how you work the knife. Yvie looks after events, so she'll be in and out all day with the client's request list.

You have five jobs, and they're on your screen displayed in order. Every one of them feeds into making tonight a success.

The one thing I'd ask? *Do not guess*. Have a proper look, take your reading, and make a note of what you see, signed with the time and your initials. I read every sheet at the end of the day, so make sure you’re being honest.

If something's not right, fix it, and if you're ever not sure, ask me. *That's what I'm here for*. If a decision could make someone poorly, I'll step in, and we'll fix it together.

Pop your name in and grab the clipboard. *Let's get started.*

---

### **2\. Task 1: Handover and fridges (about 20 seconds)**

Alright, first things first, the notes from last night. Have a read through all of them. There's one about larder two being found open at four this morning, and we don't know how long for, so keep that in mind. Some of tonight's prep is in that larder and may have been affected.

Then, we'll go round the fridges together. Open them up, have a good look inside, probe them, and give them a second to settle. Write down what you actually see, complete with the time and your initials.

That board's the first thing I look at every morning, and on a day like today, I want to know the walk-in is right before anything for the launch goes in it. The event is riding on this.

---

### **3\. Task 2: Delivery (about 25 seconds)**

Delivery's in\! Most of what's on there is for the launch, so it really matters. The driver will have a tight schedule \- *they always do* \- but I need you to take your time here.

We sign for what's on the trolley, not what's on the note. So count it, weigh it, and flag anything that hasn’t turned up.

Have a good look at the fish too. *The eyes, the gills…* Give it all a smell, and press it. If anything's not right, it goes back, and that's fine, *that's our job.*

If we’ve got too little of something, give me a shout on the radio before you sign for it. We need to know now, not at six o'clock. Then start storing the fish and meat first; the fruit can wait a minute.

---

### **4\. Task 3: Chill the batch (about 25 seconds)**

This is the main course for tonight. Beef shin, twenty-seven kilos of it. I'll do half, and you'll do half, and we'll crack on together.

Spread the beef out in the trays so it's no more than fifty millimetres deep, about the width of your hand. That's the number to remember. Spread it any thicker and it'll cool slowly, and that's where things go wrong. If you run out of trays, just ask; I can get you more.

Space the trays out in the chiller so the air can get around them. Probe into the thickest bit of meat, in the fullest tray. Don't just probe the top, and don't touch the metal.

I want you to take a reading every half hour, and make a note of what you find. The middle of the beef should be under eight degrees before it goes in the walk-in. If it's not there yet, don't stress, it just stays in a bit longer. We've got time to get this right, as long as we use it wisely.

---

### **5\. Task 4: Dietary list (about 25 seconds)**

Yvie's just popped in with the final guest list for the event. There are *three late additions*, on tables three, six, and nine. The client wants the table plan signed off this afternoon, so she needs a concrete answer from us.

Build the allergen chart first, before anything else. Go through the dishes one at a time, and check the actual recipe cards for what's in them, not just the dish name. The frangipane needs extra care: it has almonds mixed all the way through it, not just on top, so it's easy to miss if you're not paying attention.

If an allergen is mixed into a dish rather than sitting on top as a garnish, don't just remove the garnish and assume that fixes it\! Choose a different dish instead. *That's the rule.*

Once you've decided, pop it on the board. Dish, name, table. Front of house need to know what we know, and the client will ask questions.

---

### **6\. Task 5: Waste and handover (about 25 seconds)**

This is the last part, so you're nearly done. The evening team are about to take over and start serving, so everything needs to be ready for them. There are three things to do…

Waste: weigh all three tubs and write the weights on the correct row. The rice and melon from larder two should go under spoilage, not general waste.

Handover sheet: fill in what's ready for tonight, what's running low, what's in the walk-in fridge, and anything they need to keep an eye on, including the salmon, the fridge itself, and table three. When the evening team arrive, walk them through the sheet. That's all the handover needs to cover.

After that, bring me the chill record so we can talk about the beef tray. Just talk me through what happened. I'm not looking for excuses, I want to know what you'd do differently next time.

Once we've both signed it, the handover is complete. Time for the close.

---

### **7\. Close (about 35 seconds)**

That's you finished with your tasks. Well done.

Think about what you actually did today. You noticed food in a fridge that wasn't cold enough and dealt with it before anyone had to tell you. You didn't sign off on salmon that hadn't actually arrived. You kept the beef in the chiller rather than taking the easy route and calling it finished. You checked allergens properly before serving someone with a nut allergy. And you left the evening team, who are serving a hundred people tonight, a handover sheet they can actually use.

Maybe none of that stands out to you. They’re small things, but on a day like today, doing these things properly is exactly what makes an event run successfully.

Anyone can learn to cook. What I really look for is someone who checks *properly* instead of assuming, can explain why they made a certain decision, and leaves records I can trust.

*You did all of that today,* so thank you. Go and put your feet up after a challenging shift.
