## 2. What this try day should achieve

- **One-sentence purpose:**  
  After a simulated day as an apprentice dental nurse, a learner should understand that dental nursing is a busy, hands-on, patient-centred healthcare role with responsibilities before, during and after every appointment — and be able to judge honestly whether that reality appeals to them.

- **Target learner:**  
  Tempdent’s stated target audience is 16–24 year olds; its apprenticeship page speaks to school leavers, career changers and people already working in a dental setting. Springpod’s standard career simulation audience is 14–18. **TBC** which range governs the reading level and examples; this draft is written for 16+ with no prior dental knowledge. Learners are exploring the dental nursing apprenticeship pathway; some will be actively considering applying for a Tempdent apprenticeship vacancy. Apprentices must be aged 16+, so under-16s should be told this is a look ahead, not something they can apply for yet.

- **Three things learners should understand by the end:**
  1. A dental nurse’s work runs before, during and after every appointment: preparing the surgery, supporting the clinician and the patient during treatment, then decontaminating and resetting for the next patient. Chairside assistance is one part of a much broader role.
  2. Dental nursing is a patient-facing healthcare profession. Communication, reassurance, infection prevention, health and safety, confidentiality and teamwork matter as much as knowing the instruments — and it is not predominantly a cosmetic or aesthetics-based environment.
  3. You don’t need to arrive knowing how to be a dental nurse; the Level 3 apprenticeship teaches you that. You do need the right attitude: reliable, organised, curious, proactive within the limits of your role, and willing to ask rather than guess.

- **Three skills or behaviours learners should practise:**
  1. Preparing and thinking ahead — reading the day list, getting the surgery and the tray ready, and starting to anticipate what the clinician will need next.
  2. Communicating with patients and colleagues — greeting, reassuring and adapting to different patients (a nervous child, an anxious adult), and knowing when to hand a question to the dentist or mentor.
  3. Following a safety-critical routine precisely — decontamination and surgery reset in the right order, and recording what was checked.

- **What this experience must not imply:**  
  That the learner is competent or qualified to carry out any clinical, chairside or decontamination task on real patients or real instruments. That anyone can perform nursing duties in a practice before training, supervision and Hepatitis B vaccination. That completing the try day guarantees an apprenticeship place or employment. That being proactive means making clinical decisions — an apprentice is never expected to make a clinical decision they are not trained or competent to make. That dental nursing is mainly about cosmetic treatments, or that the dental nurse is “just the dentist’s assistant”.

- **Desired total experience length:**  
  30–35 minutes across six tasks (see Shift overview), within the template’s recommended range. Note that the ideation document describes the Career Simulation activity as “a 1-hour* simulation activity” — **TBC** with Tempdent whether the try day should run longer (for example by playing the 09:40 and 10:40 appointments) or sit inside a wider one-hour activity. A shorter 20-minute route (Tasks 1, 2, 3 and 6) is also possible.

## 4. Tone and learner experience

- **Three words for the employer’s voice:**  
  Clear, warm, professional. (Brand guidelines: “clean and uncomplicated, free from waffle, jargon and stuffy language”; “a balance between professional and welcoming”; use “you” and “your”.)

- **The mentor should sound like:** A senior dental nurse who remembers her own first week. Calm, practical and encouraging. Short sentences. Explains the reason behind every step (“we do it this way because…”). Praises effort specifically. Takes a decision back plainly when it belongs to her or the dentist (“that one’s mine — here’s why”). Treats asking a question as the right thing to do.
- **The mentor should never sound like:** A lecturer or an exam. Sarcastic, or alarmed by a mistake. Jargon-heavy without explaining a term. Glamorous or aesthetics-focused. Anyone who treats the nurse as the dentist’s helper. Old-fashioned (“whilst”, “amongst”) or so chatty it becomes sloppy — brand guidelines.
- **How much challenge should learners experience?**
  - [ ] Gentle introduction
  - [x] Balanced
  - [ ] Stretching but achievable

  Rationale (Tempdent): “The system should therefore teach as well as test … We don’t want candidates failing a test about dental instruments they’ve never encountered before.” Safety-critical sequences have one right order; communication choices may have several good answers, with feedback explaining why one is particularly effective.

- **How should mistakes be handled?**  
  Feedback is immediate, specific and kind. It names what the item or step is, why it matters to the patient or the team, and then lets the learner try again straight away. Safety-critical mistakes (skipping PPE, wrong wiping order, not reporting an expired drug) are corrected firmly with the consequence explained, never with alarm. No visible score is deducted for a first mistake. The mentor models “ask when unsure” as the right move, and never treats asking as failure. Where several answers are acceptable, feedback explains why one may be particularly effective rather than teaching one scripted response.

# Task storyboard

## Task 1: Set up the surgery for the morning

### A. Story setup

- **Time in the shift:** 08:15 — first thing, 45 minutes before the first patient.
- **Starting location:** Surgery 2.
- **Other locations visited:** None. (The instrument cupboard and PPE station are in the surgery.)
- **Who introduces the task:** Priya, your mentor, with the printed day list in her hand.
- **Situation:**  
  The practice has just opened. Surgery 2 was cleaned down last night but nothing has been set up or checked for today: the surfaces need wiping, the water lines have sat overnight, the emergency equipment check sheet is blank, and there is no tray ready. The first patient is Amira, aged 10, for her first filling at 09:00 with Dr Reid. Dr Reid arrives at 08:50.

- **Learner’s job:**  
  Get Surgery 2 clean, working, safe and stocked for the morning: prepare yourself correctly, wipe the surgery down in the right order, flush the water lines, check the emergency equipment and drugs, then set up the tray for Amira’s filling — ticking the check sheets as you go.

- **Why this matters in the real role:** Every patient who follows depends on the surgery being clean, working and stocked before the day starts. Emergency equipment is checked daily because a medical emergency can happen to anyone in the chair, and the moment you need the oxygen or the drugs is not the moment to discover a problem. Setting up the right tray shows you have read the day list and thought ahead — the habit Tempdent most wants learners to see.
- **What happens after the task:** Dr Reid arrives at 08:50 and reads Amira’s notes. At 08:55 Sam calls through from reception: Amira and her dad are here (Task 2).

### B. Learning purpose

- **Knowledge being tested or introduced:** What “setting up the surgery” involves. Correct clinical presentation (uniform, hair tied up if longer than collar length, jewellery removed). The 12-step clinical handwashing technique and the PPE set (gloves, mask, visor, apron). ## 9. Close-of-day screen

- **What should the learner be congratulated for?** Getting a surgery clean, checked and ready before a single patient arrived, and spotting the expired drug. Keeping a ten-year-old calm through her first filling and honouring her stop signal instantly. Resetting the surgery in the right order with a patient waiting, and not skipping a step. Prioritising sensibly and telling the right people when the morning changed. Giving an anxious adult back his sense of control, and writing him up in a way that helps the next nurse. Writing a handover someone could use.
- **Which skills or behaviours should be recapped?** Preparation and thinking ahead. Patient-centred communication with two very different patients. Infection prevention and health and safety, followed precisely under pressure. Anticipating the clinician’s needs — and knowing where an apprentice’s role stops. Teamwork: telling people, asking people. Adaptability when the day changes. Record-keeping and handover. Tempdent’s model mindset: patient first → safety first → prepare and think ahead → communicate → ask when unsure → work as part of the team → adapt → learn from feedback.
- **What should they reflect on?** Tempdent’s suggested reflection journey, in Tempdent’s order:
  1. What did I learn? Was there anything about the reality of dental nursing that surprised me?
  2. What appealed to me? Which part of a dental nurse’s day would I enjoy most?
  3. What might challenge me? Which part would I find most difficult, and why?
  4. Am I suited to it? Thinking about my own personality and strengths, what qualities do I already have that could make me a good apprentice dental nurse — and what would I need to develop?
  5. Am I prepared for an apprenticeship? How would I feel about balancing working in a dental practice with studying towards my qualification?
  6. Is this the career for me? Now that I understand both the rewarding and challenging parts of dental nursing, can I realistically see myself enjoying this career? — Yes, I’d like to explore a dental nursing apprenticeship / Maybe, I’d like to find out more first / No, I don’t think the role is right for me. (Tempdent: “No” is not a negative outcome and must not be treated as one.)
  7. What happens next? If I want to pursue dental nursing, what action am I going to take?

  Tempdent also asked for a patient-centred reflection, which can sit alongside step 3 or 4: “How would you feel about working closely with different patients every day, including people who may be nervous, in pain, vulnerable or require additional support?” The existing Springpod reflection questions Tempdent was happy to retain (main thing learned; what interests you most; how this influenced your thinking; key skills; new insights; whether it made you want to pursue the career; your career path) are covered by the wider Career Simulation’s Reflection Activity.
- **What realistic next step should be suggested?** For “Yes”: explore Tempdent’s dental nurse apprenticeship vacancies and join the talent pool if there isn’t one nearby; prepare your CV — and add this virtual work experience to it; research the dental practices you apply to; prepare for interview by being ready to talk about what you learned today. For “No”: that’s a useful thing to have found out before applying; the skills you used today — communication, organisation, following safety routines — matter in many careers.

## 13. Final notes and open questions

- **Non-negotiables:** From Tempdent’s ideation document, treated as binding unless Tempdent says otherwise:
  - Teach as well as test — no learner fails a question about an instrument they have never seen; every item explains itself.
  - No focus on Botox, fillers, facial aesthetics or cosmetic dentistry; this is a healthcare profession centred on patients.
  - Never portray the dental nurse as simply the dentist’s assistant or in a passive, support-only role.
  - Promote the apprenticeship pathway only; do not present or compare alternative routes (university, private courses).
  - Being proactive never means making a clinical decision an apprentice isn’t trained for; the model behaviour is prepare, pay attention, communicate, anticipate appropriate needs, and ask.
  - “No — this isn’t for me” is a valid and valuable outcome and is never treated as failure.
  - Imagery reflects the diversity of modern dental nursing.
  - Patient care, professionalism, infection prevention, health and safety, confidentiality, teamwork, reliability and clinical responsibility feature strongly throughout.
  - The reflection asks not just “do I want this?” but “now I understand what it involves, does it suit me?”
- **Ideas you would like explored:**
  - Swap or add a patient: the 11:00 older patient with hearing loss and limited mobility (denture fitting) or a patient with sensory needs (autism, noise-sensitive) as an alternative Task 6, or as a short extra encounter — both are in Tempdent’s own list of patient needs to include (“somebody with additional communication or sensory needs; an older patient who may require additional support”).
