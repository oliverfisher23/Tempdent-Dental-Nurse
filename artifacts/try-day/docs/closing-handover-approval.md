### P1 — Single formative waste prompt

Proposed prompt: **“Which waste would you look into, and what would you check
next?”** Invite one reason based on the weighed category, its contents or source.
No score, required word count, single correct category or new completion gate.
All three deliberate measurements and correct waste-sheet entries remain required.
The rice and melon classification is practice applying the briefing, not a
hidden test.

Proposed acceptable responses include:

- Spoilage: investigate larder 2's door/checking routine because rice and melon
  were discarded after the open-door finding. This is a follow-up, not proof
  that a particular action would have prevented all waste.
- Plate returns: find out which dishes came back and why before proposing
  portion changes. “We need more information” is valid.
- Trimmings: review preparation methods and distinguish unavoidable material
  from potentially avoidable waste. The heaviest tub is not automatically the
  most avoidable category.

Feedback should identify the evidence and the uncertainty, not rank these
responses. Do not invent costs, savings, causes or recovery uses for discarded
food. Immediate safe operation remains distinct from later waste reduction.

### P2 — Evidence and action labels

Proposed learner-facing labels: **“From your signed records”**, **“From the
team”**, and **“Proposed follow-up”**. Cite the source and show whether an action
is requested or confirmed. Earlier signed records are read-only; consulting
them must not require copying their measurements again.

Use saved evidence where available. If a fact is missing or conflicts with
supplied copy, expose that uncertainty rather than manufacturing a completed
check. A supplier contact or delivery must not be marked complete just because
the learner proposes it. Current fixed prompt copy is not, by itself, proof
that the learner or another team member performed an action.

### P3 — Priorities and permissible alternatives

Proposed groups: **“Before service”** and **“Later follow-up”**, with timing and
responsibility where relevant. Do not enforce one total ranking.

Proposed acceptable orders:

1. Larder 2 first, then table 3, then tomorrow's salmon shortage.
2. Table 3 first, then larder 2, then tomorrow's salmon shortage.
3. Salmon first in the spoken handover to arrange responsibility, provided the
   learner still clearly identifies larder 2 and table 3 as before-service
   concerns and salmon as affecting tomorrow's lunch, not tonight's event.

Supplier follow-up may need arranging promptly even though the affected meal
is tomorrow. “Later” must not mean an indefinite delay. Waste investigation can
follow the immediate service concerns.

Do not approve new fridge clearance or allergy-service rules here. Carry the
status from the relevant records and any separately approved guidance; unknown
status must not become “ready” or “safe”. No chosen priority waives a required topic.

### P4 — Finite evening-team exchange

Use authored text and structured responses, not a microphone or generative
assessment. After the learner's attempt, ask only about unresolved topics.
The question set is finite; correction attempts are not artificially limited.

| Branch | Proposed evening-team question | Required repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salmon quantity or timing missing | “How much salmon is missing, and which meal needs it?” | Communicate the 4 kg shortage and tomorrow's lunch, not tonight's event. |
| Action status unclear | “Has that happened, or are you asking us to follow it up?” | Separate a confirmed action from a request; add responsibility/timing where needed. |
| Larder 2 missing or ambiguous | “What did you find in larder 2, and what still needs checking?” | Use the signed finding and distinguish it from any proposed re-check. |
| Table 3 missing or ambiguous | “Which table is this for, and what is the dessert change?” | Communicate table 3's nut allergy and pear rather than frangipane; carry any unresolved service status forward. |
| Remaining handover heading unclear | “What is ready, and where is it for tonight?” | Complete the relevant heading using supported information, including explicit unknowns where necessary. |
| All required information resolved | Read back the supported facts, outstanding actions and timings. | Learner confirms the understanding or returns to revise it. |

Questions and feedback must not invent completed work. Read-back must reflect
the resolved handover, not a generic success message. Return to affected
questions if the learner changes a critical fact before sign-off.

### P5 — Critical-information completion checks and paraphrases

Keep all four headings and the required three topics. Replace the ten-character
test as the sole meaning check with a finite, learner-confirmed summary:

- Salmon: correct shortage, affected meal and honest follow-up status.
- Larder 2: appliance/finding identified, with unresolved checks distinguished
  from completed checks; do not enforce a fixed reading/time against different
  signed evidence.
- Table 3: table and nut-allergy requirement identified, pear rather than
  frangipane communicated, and no invented preparation/service clearance.
- Prepared items and walk-in contents: supported information under the retained
  headings, without hardcoded tray counts, dates or readings contradicting
  the saved record or the pending capacity/cooling decision.
- Delivery: relevant questions repaired and recipient understanding explicitly
  confirmed before the handover is considered delivered.

Structured responses provide a no-keyword, no-second-transcription route to
demonstrating the facts. Free-text phrasing is not judged by character count
or exact matching. Contradictory information requires clarification, not an
automatic pass because the right word appears somewhere.

Proposed equivalent wording includes:

| Meaning | Example paraphrases to accept |
| --- | --- |
| Salmon short by 4 kg | “Four kilos missing”; “8 kg arrived out of the 12 kg ordered” |
| Affects tomorrow's lunch | “For lunch tomorrow, not tonight”; “Tonight doesn't use it; tomorrow's lunch does” |
| Larder check requested, not complete | “Larder 2 needs checking before service”; “Ask the evening team to re-check fridge 2 before service” |
| Table 3 dessert change | “Table 3, nut allergy: pear, not frangipane”; “Give table three the poached pear instead of the almond tart because of the nut allergy” |

These are examples of equivalent meanings, not canned full answers or an
exhaustive accepted-string list. “Salmon is sorted”, “the fridge is fine” or
“dessert changed” alone do not convey the required facts. Feedback must explain
what is missing and allow correction before final sign-off.
