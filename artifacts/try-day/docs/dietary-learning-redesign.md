The learning outcome is: **use ingredient evidence to identify a conflict, justify a suitable proposed change, recognise what still needs checking, and leave the evening team a usable instruction.** Ingredient matching is not the same as permission to serve someone with an allergy.

3. **Disclosure:** approve moving answer-bearing explanations until after a first decision or a requested hint. If those explanations must stay upfront, describe and assess the activity as **coached practice**, not independent discovery.

## 3. Annotated textual storyboard

All learner-facing wording below is **proposed copy for approval**, not a replacement for signed-off strings.

### A. Understand the job and the menu

- **Purpose:** establish why the chart matters before asking for data entry.
- **Opening copy:** “Check what's in each dish. Your chart will help us decide which dishes need changing for tonight's guests. Then explain the changes to Yvie and the evening team.”
- **Learner action:** read the event context and inspect the five menu items; open each ingredient list in place.
- **Evidence:** standard starter, main and dessert; the vegetarian main and available alternative dessert; existing recipe lists and made/availability notes. Keep the 100-guest product launch and current timetable, without adding a countdown.
- **Decision:** distinguish the standard menu from alternatives. Proposed starting main/dessert plan is beef and frangipane for review, **subject to employer confirmation**, not a claim that the existing app stores each guest's original order.
- **Feedback:** directions explain the next action, not the allergens or guest solution. Full guest requirements are not yet shown.

### B. Inspect ingredients and build the chart

- **Purpose:** practise attention to all parts of a dish, not just its name.
- **Learner action:** select a dish, inspect its ingredients, deliberately mark allergens present, identify any missing information and confirm review of the remaining categories.
- **Evidence:** the active recipe beside its row, all fourteen categories, an optional ingredient-to-category reference, and any employer-approved supplier details.
- **Decision:** which categories are supported by the supplied evidence; which questions remain unresolved?
- **Feedback:** show “Not started”, “In progress”, “Reviewed” or “Reviewed — question open”. Never turn an untouched cell into evidence of absence. No seventy written explanations.

### C. Review the chart with Terence

- **Purpose:** make the chart dependable as an ingredient reference.
- **Learner action:** deliberately request review after completing each row's confirmation; correct highlighted rows and request review again.
- **Evidence:** the learner's entries and the same ingredient sources, with no completed answer grid.
- **Decision:** revise unsupported marks or missed allergens; distinguish a recipe conclusion from a supplier/preparation question.
- **Feedback:** initially name the dish and source to revisit, not every missing tick. Confirm “Matches the supplied ingredients” rather than “Allergy-safe”. Reviewed unknowns remain visible. Once known ingredient entries are accurate and all five rows are reviewed, reveal the detailed guest list.

### D. Compare guest requests with the planned dishes

- **Purpose:** turn the chart into evidence for a decision.
- **Learner action:** read the function sheet (the event's guest and service information); for each guest inspect the planned main/dessert against the completed chart.
- **Evidence:** guest name, table and stated requirement alongside the two relevant chart rows; one action returns to the full matrix or opens either recipe without losing the guest.
- **Decision:** flag an incompatible item, or explicitly say “No conflict identified in this main and dessert”; then choose **Keep / Swap / Ask Terence**.
- **Feedback:** a known conflict stops confirmation. A request for clarification is legitimate, not a wrong answer. No automatic choice of an alternative.

### E. Work through Priya's dessert

- **Purpose:** assess the main reasoning challenge, including the limit of a recipe-only check.
- **Learner action:** flag the planned frangipane, select both ground almonds and pistachios as evidence, explain why removing the garnish is not a solution, then inspect the available pear dessert and preparation information.
- **Evidence:** frangipane ingredients, its already-prepared status and factual information that almonds are mixed through it; pear ingredients including milk in the ice cream; twelve available portions; only employer-confirmed preparation/supplier statements.
- **Decision:** propose the pear instead of frangipane; retain the milk declaration; ask about any unresolved allergy/preparation concern before service approval.
- **Feedback:** reject garnish removal and service while concerns remain open. Prompt evidence review before offering stronger hints. Do not invent where the pistachios sit: the almond mixture alone establishes why picking off visible nuts cannot fix the dish.

### F. Contrast Tom and Anna

- **Purpose:** show that checking does not always require changing a dish.
- **Learner action:** for Tom, flag beef and compare the Wellington; check dessert separately. For Anna, review the planned main and dessert despite “None stated”.
- **Evidence:** Tom's vegetarian requirement; beef and Wellington ingredients; the existing “Vegetarian main” designation; Anna's lack of a stated restriction.
- **Decision:** Tom can propose Wellington and keep frangipane on the supplied evidence, subject to any unresolved supplier questions. Anna can legitimately keep beef and frangipane. Vegetarian does not mean vegan.
- **Feedback:** praise the evidence, not the number of swaps. Show an explicit scope notice: **“You're checking mains and desserts here. Tom's starter still needs a separate decision.”** Do not approve his fish starter or invent another one.

### G. Explain, communicate and sign off

- **Purpose:** leave instructions someone else can act on.
- **Learner action:** write a short reason, check the board entry for every actual change, review the remaining checks and deliberately post/sign off.
- **Evidence:** the reviewed chart, guest decisions, original and replacement dishes, and separate approved preparation responses.
- **Decision:** are the changes correctly identified, justified and communicated; is anything still on hold?
- **Feedback:** identify missing or contradictory fields and preserve the learner's draft. Allow editing and retry before sign-off. Once signed off, retain the existing read-only record and ordered progression.

## 4. Menu-first workspace and matrix rules

### Layout and access

Use the screenshot only for **dishes down the side and allergen headings across the top**. Do not import its TouchBistro branding, icons as the sole labels, or nine-column taxonomy.

- **Desktop:** five-row matrix with readable text headings, persistent dish labels and an adjacent ingredient pane tied to the selected row. Sauces, fillings, sides and garnishes stay within that dish's evidence. Keep the legend and review control visible; horizontal scrolling must not separate cells from their row/column identity.
- **Mobile:** one dish card at a time with name/course, ingredients and all fourteen labelled controls in the same view/scroll context. Preserve the selected dish and scroll position when opening the reference or comparing guest choices. An overview shows five review statuses. Do not shrink a desktop grid to illegibility.
- **Keyboard and assistive technology:** native labelled controls, table row/column headers on desktop, full dish/category accessible names, visible focus, Space/Enter operation, textual status announcements and focus returned to the triggering control. Errors must not depend on colour or shaking. Keep the next-step guide reachable and preserve normal zoom/reflow.
- The notebook remains optional. No ingredient transcription, mandatory room search or repeated room change is required to do the reasoning.

### Coverage: five dishes, fourteen categories

Retain all five dishes: **smoked haddock and leek tart, watercress; braised beef shin, horseradish mash, glazed carrots, red wine jus; wild mushroom, spinach and ricotta Wellington; pistachio and raspberry frangipane tart, crème fraîche; poached pear, vanilla ice cream.**

Retain these fourteen labels: **Celery; Cereals containing gluten; Crustaceans; Eggs; Fish; Lupin; Milk; Molluscs; Mustard; Nuts (tree nuts); Peanuts; Sesame; Soya; Sulphur dioxide and sulphites.** Keep nuts and peanuts separate; do not replace “Cereals containing gluten” with “Wheat”.

### Explicit legend and row confirmation

| Display | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Empty / Not reviewed | No conclusion yet; never “absent”. |
| Present | Deliberately marked by the learner from ingredient evidence. |
| — Reviewed, not identified | The learner has explicitly confirmed checking the remaining categories against the supplied ingredients; not an allergen-free claim. |
| ? Check needed | A specific ingredient/category question remains unresolved. General supplier or preparation questions sit alongside the row, not as invented positive allergen marks. |

Use one deliberate confirmation per row: **“I've checked all fourteen categories against these ingredients. I've marked those present and noted anything I need to check.”** A learner-owned confirmation makes the remaining cells “Reviewed, not identified”; it does not calculate or fill in correct answers. “Reviewed” and “accurate” are separate states. Editing an unsigned row removes its review confirmation and requires dependent decisions/board entries to be checked again.

Unknown supplier details must not force guessing. The matrix can be reviewed **against the supplied recipe** with open questions attached; this supports later guest reasoning but cannot grant service approval. Do not treat a supplier “may contain” statement as either a confirmed recipe ingredient or permission to serve.

### Simple reference, not an answer key

Offer short general mappings: almonds/pistachios → nuts; peanuts → peanuts, a separate category; ricotta/cream/butter → milk; wheat pastry → cereals containing gluten; egg wash → eggs. Let learners select a term for a plain-language explanation. Do not label every recipe line with its answer or pre-highlight all correct cells. Source-specific hints are available only when requested or after a failed review.

For a finite exercise, the preferred approval is a complete employer-reviewed evidence pack plus an authored response to the relevant preparation question. If the employer instead wants ingredient-matching practice only, explicitly agree that narrower completion claim and reconcile the signed-off wording before implementation. Do not ship an unresolvable task or quietly equate exercise completion with service clearance.

## 6. Guest decision records and worked examples

### What each main/dessert decision retains

- Guest name/table and the exact confirmed requirement.
- Planned item; item flagged, or an explicit “No conflict identified”.
- Evidence selected by the learner from their chart and the ingredient/request source.
- **Keep / Swap / Ask**, proposed final item if known, and one brief learner-written reason.
- A structured reason category, such as ingredient conflict, vegetarian conflict, no stated conflict or information missing; this supports reliable checks without judging prose by keywords.
- Separate outstanding question, approved response/source and preparation/service status. An Ask may lead to a justified Keep or Swap after clarification; it is not a forced wrong turn.

Keep examples below in the design specification or post-attempt discussion, not as pre-filled learner responses.

## 7. Feedback, support and assessment

### Correction and graduated hints

| Situation | First feedback and retry | Stronger optional support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incomplete row | “Check the remaining categories before asking Terence to review this dish.” Return to that row without losing marks. | Explain the legend and point to the row confirmation; do not complete it. |
| Recipe mismatch | “Check the Wellington's pastry, filling and glaze against your row.” Learner revisits evidence and edits. | Point to a relevant ingredient, then explain its category mapping if still needed. |
| Frangipane selected for Priya | “Stop — this choice conflicts with Priya's allergy. Check the tart's ingredients and your chart.” Block confirmation, retain the attempt for correction. | First: inspect filling as well as topping. Next: ground almonds are nuts mixed into the tart; removing a garnish does not remove them. Only a final requested hint directs comparison with the pear recipe, including its milk and preparation checks. |
| Beef selected for Tom | “Stop — compare the main's ingredients with Tom's vegetarian request.” | Point to beef, then direct comparison with the existing vegetarian-main recipe; never select it automatically. |
| Pear called allergen-free | “Check the vanilla ice cream against the milk column.” Require correction of the claim/evidence. | Explain that a dish can fit one request and still contain another allergen. |
| Preparation unknown | “The recipe isn't a preparation check. Ask Terence before approving service.” Keep the proposed dish but mark service on hold. | Show only the employer-approved source or response; no invented assurance. |
| Board incomplete or inconsistent | Name the missing field or mismatched dish; keep the draft editable. | Offer field prompts, not a completed answer to copy. |

The hint ladder is **source → component → mapping/explanation → explicit comparison**. Learners can retry without penalty, a timer, points or a forced restart. Once an answer has been supplied by a strong hint, treat subsequent work as supported practice; do not claim independent discovery.

### What counts as success

- **Attention to evidence:** accurate five-row ingredient matrix; explicit review of all fourteen categories; appropriate use of ingredient and guest-request sources; unresolved details acknowledged.
- **Reasoned decision:** original conflict identified, relevant evidence selected, a coherent keep/swap/ask decision and brief reason. Priya's record addresses almonds and pistachios and why garnish removal fails. Tom and Anna demonstrate different legitimate outcomes.
- **Safety boundary:** no known conflicting dish approved; no unresolved preparation concern labelled cleared; milk retained in the pear row.
- **Usable communication:** every actual change identifies guest, table, original/replacement and dietary reason; preparation status is separate and consistent.

Use deterministic checks for structured ingredient/evidence selections, dish compatibility, required decision fields, review states and board consistency. A brief free-text reason is retained and checked for presence, **not semantically “passed” by an exact phrase or AI**. Structured evidence/reason categories carry the automated assessment; an educator can assess the written explanation qualitatively with the criteria above. Do not claim a non-empty sentence proves reasoning quality.
