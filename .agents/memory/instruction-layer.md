---
name: Instruction layer and overlay stack
description: Where the try-day app may talk about its own controls, how the opener and guide bar split that job, and the rule for stacked overlays sharing Escape and focus.
---

- COPY.md rule 4 ("describe the kitchen, not the software") has one agreed exception: the instruction layer. The "How this works" tiles and pattern cards, the How text behind a workspace opener's "How do I do this?", and the step guide instruction may name the gesture and its button alternative. Dialogue, feedback and labels still may not.
  **Why:** a client review found learners could not tell what to do on the chart, chiller, waste and probe screens, and the guide described controls that did not exist. The client asked for explicit control instructions.
  **How to apply:** one sentence, name only controls on the screen right now, never reveal an answer, keep the words in the task's content file. Every disabled or locked control gets a visible one-line reason that stays put (no timers).
- One instruction layer per screen (decided after the September 2026 accessibility audit). The guide bar is the single statement of the current step and names its control with the outcome ("Mark the allergen chart"), never the mechanism ("Open the chart") and never "Tap". The opener collapsed to one line: what to do, live progress count, one "How do I do this?" button holding How and Done-when, and the briefing video. Workspaces have no second "next" button; the guide bar moves the learner on.
  **Why:** with a three-part opener, the guide bar and header next-buttons, three places told the learner what to do and could drift apart. The client's earlier ask for explicit control instructions still holds, so the guide bar stays always visible and the How text was moved, not removed. This is a tradeoff to flag if the client asks where the How line went.
  **How to apply:** new copy for a step goes in the guide (`content/guides/`), and the opener's How/Done-when in the scene content file. Guide action labels may equal the hotspot label they point at; tests must scope room lookups to `main` (the guide lives in the HUD above it). "Check my work" is the only check-button label; results go through the shared check feedback block.
- Overlays inside the kitchen (close-ups, map, notebook, job card, small modals) share one stack; only the topmost answers Escape and Tab. Any new overlay must register with it, and any Escape handler must check it is on top.
  **Why:** the step guide sits inside the parents' focus traps, so a help card can open above the map, notebook or job card; a review found one Escape closing both layers when a handler skipped the check.
- Close-ups stay non-modal (`aria-modal="false"`, no `inert`) and are named by the visible workspace title (`data-dialog-title`, else the first h1/h2), set before focus moves in.
  **Why:** the Terence dialogue live region sits inside `main` and the header tools must stay usable over a close-up (notebook and chill record copy across), so keyboard parity comes from widening the Tab cycle to the HUD instead. Naming from "the first heading" picked a subsection ("Tonight's menu") on the function sheet, and naming after focus arrived made the name change mid-announcement.
- Hotspot labels show briefly on arrival, but the reason under a locked spot waits for hover, focus or a tap, and labels near either edge hang from the spot's side. Once tapped, the reason stays until the spot unlocks or is tapped again, and is announced.
  **Why:** on a 390 px phone the arrival labels plus reasons overlapped and ran off the left edge of the room; a 4 s timer then hid the reason before slow readers finished it.
- axe scans from the learner run (`AXE=1`) must wait for entrance fades and scan only an open true modal; otherwise half-faded text and the page behind a modal show up as dozens of false contrast failures.
