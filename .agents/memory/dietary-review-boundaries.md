---
name: Dietary review boundaries
description: Approval and safety boundaries for using the dietary learning-design recommendation in future work.
---

- The later request to implement all task redesigns authorises interface implementation, but is not employer confirmation of allergy, preparation or service evidence.
  **Why:** the original review-only commission was superseded by an explicit implementation request on 2026-09-18; the missing employer evidence was not supplied with it.
  **How to apply:** do not treat the earlier specification-only status as a continuing ban on UI work. Preserve protected copy and distinguish recipe-based proposals from permission to serve; obtain employer review before learner rollout.

- Keep ingredient matching separate from employer-approved preparation/service clearance.
  **Why:** the learning challenge should require evidence and reasoning without implying that a simplified recipe proves freedom from cross-contact. Inventing supplier declarations or a chef's assurance would remove the uncertainty by fabricating evidence.
  **How to apply:** unresolved preparation questions remain a hold, not a safe-to-serve result. Completing the interface records a proposed menu and communication exercise only; employer approval of that narrower scope and the protected wording remains a rollout requirement.

- The dietary content decisions were taken by the reviewer under delegation from the project owner acting as the employer's representative (2026-09-18) and live in the redesign document's decision register (section 9). The agreed claim is the narrower one: ingredient planning against the supplied recipe cards, coached practice, and a visible "preparation check — pending, ask Terence" hold that never turns green.
  **Why:** the representative had no employer evidence to supply and asked the reviewer to decide; scenario parameters (starting plan, pear allocation, guest wording) can be set by a designer, but supplier declarations, cross-contact procedures and chef's safety assurances cannot be invented without fabricating safety evidence. The representative's job role was not recorded, so treat the register as the working approval and keep the employer review before rollout.
  **How to apply:** build to the register rather than re-asking the same questions; keep tree nuts and peanuts as separate exclusions; never add a "safe to serve" confirmation; reopen the open evidence items only if the employer later wants a service-clearance claim.

- A working Task 4 build is not the employer's approval of its allergy content.
  **Why:** the register was agreed under delegation without employer evidence, so the outstanding employer review is a rollout condition, not something a finished interface satisfies.
  **How to apply:** extend the built flow rather than re-planning it; any new wording that could read as service clearance still needs the employer, not a designer.

- Function-sheet accommodation notes are part of the ingredient evidence and must agree with the matrix truth.
  **Why:** a "beef without jus thickening" note for a coeliac guest implied gluten that the beef row did not carry; a sharp learner would read the chart as contradicting its own sheet.
  **How to apply:** when changing any recipe card or allergen row, re-read the existing-requirements notes and keep both consistent, or change both together.
